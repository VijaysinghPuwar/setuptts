## SetupTTS v1.9.0

**Kokoro HD: lifelike offline voices, free, on Mac and Windows.**

---

### New

- **41 Kokoro HD voices in 7 languages.** Kokoro-82M is a free (Apache-2.0) neural voice model that sounds far closer to a real narrator than the other offline voices. It covers American and British English, Spanish, French, Hindi, Italian and Brazilian Portuguese. Open **+ Get voices**, and the Kokoro voices are listed first, best-rated first: **Heart**, **Bella**, **Nicole**, **Emma**, and the rest. They appear under **Offline** in the voice picker, marked **Offline HD**.
- **Small downloads after the first.** The first Kokoro voice also downloads the shared Kokoro model (325 MB). Each voice after that is under 1 MB. Removing your last Kokoro voice removes the model too.
- **Nothing to install.** Kokoro runs on the same speech engine the offline voices already use, so it works the same on macOS and Windows with no extra setup. Every file is downloaded from a fixed version and checked before use, so a broken or tampered download is never installed.
- **NVIDIA graphics cards (Windows, beta).** With GPU support turned on, Kokoro voices use the card like the other offline voices. If the card fails, Kokoro carries on on the processor, without turning the card off for the other offline voices.
- **Credits shown where needed.** The French voice Siwis was trained on a corpus that asks for credit when audio is published; its tooltip in **Get voices** says so.

### How it sounds right

- Text is spoken in pieces of a few sentences, the length the model handles best, with even pauses between them, so long books don't drift in speed or loudness.
- Kokoro adds about 0.8 s of silence around every piece; that is trimmed so joins sound like natural sentence breaks.
- Half-precision Kokoro models are smaller, but in testing they returned silence for about one passage in four. SetupTTS uses the full-precision model, which returned none. If a passage ever comes back invalid anyway, it is spoken in two halves instead of being left as a gap.

### Fixes

- **Remove** in **Get voices** no longer fails silently when a voice is in use. On Windows it now explains that a conversion is using the voice. This also applies to Piper voices.
- **Remove** is unavailable while a download is running.

### Not included

Kokoro's Japanese and Chinese voices are not offered. They need pronunciation data that SetupTTS does not include, and without it they read most words wrongly.

### Tested

- **A whole web novel, start to finish.** 1.75 million characters (195 chapters) ran through the same job pipeline the app uses, with Heart, on a MacBook Pro M5. The result was **29 h 24 min of audio in 3 h 51 min**, about 7.6× realtime. Memory levelled off at 2.4 GB and stayed there for the last three hours. The finished file has no silence longer than 2 seconds, and nothing was logged as a warning. Speech recognition on one-minute samples from the start, the end and three points in between matched the book's text, apart from character names.
- **No gibberish.** 40 passages from that book, chosen at random plus the ones with the most quotes, ellipses and numbers, were each read by Heart, Michael and Emma. Speech recognition turned them back into text. The average word error was 3.7%, no passage was above 18%, and every difference was a name or how a number is written ("2000" against "2,000").
- **Every voice, and hostile text.** All 41 voices spoke a passage in their own language. Heart and Emma also read markup, emoji, run-on text, long number lists and mixed scripts, at speeds from −50% to +100%. Two 30 000-character jobs ran side by side. All 53 cases passed.
- **The packaged apps.** On both macOS and Windows, CI downloads the Kokoro model inside the built app and speaks a sentence with it. Every artifact (DMG, zip, installer, portable) also checks Kokoro's pronunciation step.
- 818 automated tests pass on macOS (69 new) and 815 on Windows. Kokoro on an NVIDIA card has been tested with simulated failures but not yet on a real card, like the rest of the GPU support (beta).
